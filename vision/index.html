Next: memory for machines that act.

Where the thread leads: from memory banks for video to memory for agents that act in the physical world, and the work in progress.

My work so far has circled one question: what should a machine keep from what it has seen? In video perception the answer was a memory bank that stores features from the entire video and reads them cheaply (MAMBA), and prompts that carry context from neighbouring frames into the backbone (STPN). In generation it was learning the structure of human motion well enough to produce signing avatars from text (Neural Sign Actors, with colleagues at Imperial College London).

Physical AI raises the stakes. A robot following an instruction must remember where it put something, what it has already tried and what has changed, over minutes rather than frames, and on a fixed compute budget. Many vision-language-action policies still act mostly on the current observation and instruction.

Perception with memory, generation with structure, action with both.
