I build memory for machines that see, and next, for machines that act.

I’m a Lecturer at Queen’s University Belfast, working where computer vision meets machine learning. My work covers video object detection, spatio-temporal representation learning, data-efficient generative models and 3D human motion, and has appeared at CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, ICML and AAAI. I did my PhD at Queen’s with Dr. Yang Hua, Prof. Hui Wang and Prof. Neil Robertson.

Plate I · AAAI 2021 · Video object detection

MAMBA

Multi-level aggregation via memory bank

Video detectors improve the current frame by attending to other frames. Sliding windows and queues attend to everything they store and forget whole frames at once. MAMBA keeps a memory bank that is updated feature by feature, so it can hold knowledge from the entire video, and it reads the bank through a small sampled key set to stay fast.

Not the 2023 state-space model: this MAMBA dates from 2021.

Plate II · ICCV 2023 · Video understanding

STPN

Spatio-temporal prompting network

Blur, defocus and occlusion degrade single frames. Instead of bolting a heavy, task-specific integration module onto the backbone, STPN predicts a handful of video prompts from neighbouring frames and prepends them to the current frame’s patch embeddings. One backbone then serves detection, instance segmentation and tracking.

Plate III · CVPR 2024 · 3D humans

Neural Sign Actors

A diffusion model for 3D sign language production from text

A diffusion model that turns English text directly into 3D signing avatars (body, hands and face). It is trained on a 3D reconstruction of How2Sign with SMPL-X, and denoises motion with an anatomically informed graph network on the skeleton.

Led by V. Baltatzis and R. A. Potamias (Imperial College London), with E. Ververas, J. Deng and S. Zafeiriou.
